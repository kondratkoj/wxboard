# wxboard

Personal preflight weather board. Enter one or more airports and get, for that area:

- **Airport cards:** flight category, ceiling, visibility, wind, altimeter, observation age, and the worst TAF category in the next 6 hours
- **METAR and TAF:** raw text, plus a TAF table split into periods with a flight category for each (TEMPO/PROB shown separately)
- **Stations in area:** every reporting station inside the radius or route corridor
- **Advisories:** SIGMETs/AIRMETs, Center Weather Advisories and G-AIRMETs that touch the area
- **PIREPs** from the last 3 hours, with urgent reports flagged
- **TFRs** that overlap or come within the radius
- **NOTAMs,** split into Active and Upcoming (needs FAA NMS access, see below). **Show: Critical / Caution / Non-critical** buttons pick which levels are listed, in any combination (remembered per browser). The levels are wxboard's own reading of the NOTAM text. The filter only changes the NOTAM list: the alert strip and the shift outlook bars are unaffected, and the alert strip's critical-NOTAM link turns Critical back on.
- **Winds aloft** over each airport, now and +3/6/12/24 hr, with a **max altitude** cutoff (3,000–39,000 ft, remembered per browser). 1,000-ft steps when the cutoff is 12,000 ft or lower. Winds come from the NOAA GFS / RRFS models via Open-Meteo and are only fetched again when Open-Meteo reports a new model run (every 6 h, plus a 10-minute settle time), using its free metadata files; the panel shows which GFS run you're looking at. Requests to Open-Meteo are limited to 2 at a time server-wide.
- **METAR trend:** the last 6 hours of observations for each airport as colored tiles, with ceiling/visibility trend arrows
- **Minimums:** current conditions, TAF periods and the trend are checked against your choice of minimums, with Green / Amber / Amber Critical / Red bands. Day vs night is worked out per airport from civil twilight (14 CFR 1.1).
  - **Part 135 HAA** (default): the 14 CFR 135.609(a) table (day / night unaided / night NVG; local or cross-country). **Terrain is automatic:** each airport's position is checked against the designated mountainous areas of 14 CFR Part 95 Subpart B (Eastern US, Western US, Alaska, Hawaii, Puerto Rico, minus their exception areas), so the mountainous or non-mountainous row is picked per airport. Cards show **MTN** when the mountainous row is used and **MTN edge** within 10 nm of a Part 95 line; in route mode the dialog lists legs that pass through a mountainous area. A fixed Always non-mountainous / Always mountainous choice is still available.
  - **Part 91 VFR**: the basic VFR minimums of 14 CFR 91.155 for the airspace at each airport's surface, from the FAA's airspace data: inside a Class B/C/D/E surface area a 1,000 ft ceiling and 3 SM (91.155(c), (d)); in Class G below 1,200 ft AGL, helicopter ½ SM day / 1 SM night and airplane 1 SM day / 3 SM night (91.155(a)). Class G has no ceiling minimum, so a low ceiling there shows Amber or Amber Critical but never Red. Part-time surface areas (part-time tower, E2 by NOTAM) are treated as in effect and tagged *PT*. Below basic VFR in a surface area, the badge's hover mentions Special VFR (91.157). Cards show the surface class (B, C, D, E, G).
  - The **Minimums** dialog has four folding sections (remembered per browser): *Color bands* (the margins, drawn as a colored ladder with the resulting numbers, warning if Amber Critical is set wider than Amber), *Which minimums* (Part 135 or Part 91 and their options), *This brief* (each airport's terrain or airspace, its limit and where each color starts, for now / day / night) and the regulation table with the rows in use highlighted. A reading aid, not a flight release.
- **Shift outlook:** hour-by-hour timeline for your shift (start time, 6–24 h): TAF and LAMP conditions colored by your minimums, winds at a chosen altitude, precip chance, day/night, and bars for SIGMETs, CWAs, G-AIRMETs, TFRs and red/amber NOTAMs, with a one-line summary on top. **Worst case from: TAF + LAMP / TAF only** (remembered per browser) sets what the worst case is built from; the current METAR always counts for the current hour. Each airport card's **Shift** badge shows the worst level, its hours and which source first showed it (e.g. *Red 1200–1900L · LAMP*), and the hover says when (or whether) the TAF reaches it. LAMP is NWS automated guidance; the FAA treats it as supplementary to the TAF (InFO 17006).
- **NWS area forecast** in plain English
- **Map** with two tabs, remembered per browser: **AWC GFA** (default) embeds the Aviation Weather Center Graphical Forecasts for Aviation, centered on your area: observations, radar, satellite, gridded clouds / flight category / icing / turbulence / winds, advisories and the Low Altitude (former HEMS Tool) mode via its helicopter button. It only re-centers when you brief a different area, so your own pan, zoom, layers and time survive refreshes; its settings are kept by the GFA itself, and **Open in new tab** opens the same view on aviationweather.gov. Embedding isn't a documented AWC feature, so if the AWC ever blocks it, use that link or the other tab. **wxboard map** draws your radius circle or route corridor, METARs colored by flight category, PIREPs, TFRs and advisory outlines (following the hazard filter). Switching tabs doesn't reset either map.
- **Areas of operation:** type airports and click **Save** to keep them as a named chip under the header; **Edit areas** renames them, changes airports / radius / route, reorders, removes and picks a **Default** (★) that is briefed when you open wxboard. With sign-ins on they are saved with the account (`/api/areas`), so they follow the pilot to any device; with sign-ins off they stay in the browser. Areas saved in a browser before sign-ins are imported into the account once. Without a default, wxboard reopens the last brief you ran in that browser.
- **Jump to:** a row of quick links in the sticky header, one per panel in the order they sit on the page (with each panel's count). Clicking one scrolls to that panel, opens it if it was minimized and flashes it. Minimized panels show as dashed links. This row is always shown and doesn't fold.
- **Other sites:** a row of links on the left of the layout toolbar, each in its site's color (AWC GFA, ForeFlight Web, Windy, College of DuPage satellite/radar, 1800wxbrief, SPC convective outlooks, NHC tropical weather outlook). Each pilot can add (with a color), remove and reorder their own under **Edit** (saved per browser); `{lat}`, `{lon}` and `{id}` in an address are filled in from the first airport of the current brief. The row folds like everything else. Links added to the defaults later (e.g. SPC/NHC) are appended once to a list a pilot already edited; removing them sticks.

Areas: a single airport gives a circle around it. Several airports give a circle around each one. Tick **Route** to get a corridor along the airports in order. Save areas as quick-pick chips. The URL updates as you go, so bookmarks work too. The page refreshes every 5 minutes.

### How NOTAMs are fetched (FAA production limits)

The FAA allows **one data pull every 3 minutes** (more gets HTTP 429) and **one full download per 24 hours**. Pulling the DOMESTIC, FDC and MILITARY categories once counts as one full download. So wxboard never asks the FAA per search. Instead it:

- keeps a **local copy of all US NOTAMs** (domestic, FDC and military) in `data/`;
- re-downloads the full set **once every 24 hours**;
- pulls **changes every 3 min 10 s** (new, updated, cancelled, replaced), with the 1-minute overlap the FAA recommends;
- **survives restarts**: the copy is saved to disk, restarts don't trigger another full download, and the first update after a restart waits out the 3-minute spacing;
- on a 429 it waits twice as long before trying again; a failed category download is retried after an hour.

## Notes and limits

- If a weather source can't be refreshed, wxboard keeps using its last good copy (up to 3 hours) instead of blanking the panel, but it always says so: a red **Old data** chip in the alert strip names the source and the copy's age, and the server log prints `Source problem:` when a source starts failing and `Source OK again:` when it recovers. The airport cards always use the newest METAR any request returned, so a card can't show an older report than the METAR trend.
- **Regulation watch** (`lib/regs.js`): every hover on a minimums badge cites the regulation behind it (e.g. *14 CFR 91.155(c),(d)*, *135.609(a)*, *95.13*, *1.1* for night). Once a day (and ~20 s after start) the server asks eCFR, the official online CFR, for each cited section's latest amendment date and current text. If a section was amended after wxboard was checked against it (2026-11-21), removed, or no longer contains the phrases wxboard relies on, the page shows a red **Regulation changed** chip in the alert strip, a ⚠ on every badge that uses it, and the Minimums dialog's regulation list says which; the server log prints `REGULATION CHANGED`. If eCFR can't be reached for 7 days, an amber chip says so. After reviewing a real change, update the code and the section's `basedOn` / `phrases` in `lib/regs.js`. § 1.1 is amended often for unrelated definitions, so only its definition of night is watched. The 135.609(a) table is an image in the CFR; its image name is watched, so a replaced table is caught.
- **Part-time surface areas** (`lib/sources/nasr.js`, `public/airspace-hours.js`): the FAA's published hours for each part-time Class B/C/D/E surface area (NASR CLS_ARSP, the Chart Supplement's "AIRSPACE: CLASS D svc … other times CLASS E" line) are downloaded once per 28-day cycle from nfdc.faa.gov and kept in `data/nasr/`. With the airport's time zone (from the NWS points API) the page works out which class is in effect at every hour: the card shows the class now and the next change (e.g. *D →E 2300*), and the shift outlook and TAF rows use the class in effect at each hour. Times without Z are local at the airport; Z times are UTC, and Z‡ ("Z++" in NASR) means one hour earlier in daylight time (checked against the Chart Supplement for FSM: 0530–2300 local = 1130–0500Z‡). About 84% of the published lines are understood (daily, weekday and seasonal schedules); the rest ("BY NOTAM", "DURING TWR HRS", holiday rules, military schedules) are treated as in effect, which uses the more restrictive minimums, and the hover shows the FAA text. Holiday closures are reported, not applied. NOTAMs at the airport that mention the tower or airspace get a **NOTAM** tag: they can change the hours and wxboard doesn't read them for that.
- Surface airspace (`lib/sources/airspace.js`): one small query per airport/station position to the FAA's Class_Airspace feature service (services6.arcgis.com, FAA Aeronautical Information Services): "which Class B/C/D/E surface areas contain this point?". Answers are cached 24 h per position and an old answer is used for up to 7 days if the service fails; error replies are never cached, and after a "Too many requests" the service is left alone for 2 minutes. At most 3 requests at a time. (An earlier version asked for every surface area in the brief's whole box, which a wide brief like KDTW + KASE made too big for the service.) A place whose lookup failed uses the surface-area minimums and its card shows *Airspace?*.
- Mountainous areas (`public/terrain.js`): the boundary points are copied from the eCFR text of 14 CFR 95.13–95.21 (retrieved 2026-11-21) and a test checks every point against it (`test/part95-reg.json`). Where the regulation says "along the Canadian/Mexican border", "from the Pacific coastline" or "the State of Alaska", the line is approximated, so airports right on a border or coast can come out wrong. Airports within 10 nm of a regulation line are flagged.
- Not an FAA-approved briefing source. Use it alongside an official briefing.
- Flight categories use the standard thresholds: LIFR <500 ft / <1 SM, IFR <1000 / <3, MVFR 1000–3000 / 3–5, VFR above that. Station categories come from AWC when provided.
- In route mode, hazard and station checks use the true corridor. The wxboard map draws a line plus circles at each airport as an approximation; the GFA is centered on the whole route but doesn't draw it.
- Max 12 airports per brief and 40 stations listed. Radius is limited to 5–300 nm.
- LAMP gives ceiling and visibility as category ranges (e.g. 500–900 ft). The outlook judges both ends against your minimums and stripes the cell when they disagree. LAMP is statistical guidance, not a TAF.
- NOTAM bars in the outlook show a NOTAM's overall validity; a daily schedule (e.g. 1400–2000 DLY) is in the tooltip, not drawn hour by hour.
- Winds aloft: NOAA's FB winds/temps aloft tables were discontinued 2026-11-03 with the NAM model retirement (NWS SCN 26-47). wxboard now uses NOAA GFS model winds at pressure levels via Open-Meteo, interpolated to each altitude. These are model output, not an official NWS winds aloft forecast. Check them with `node tools/winds-test.js`.
- NOTAM sync was tested against a simulated NMS server (login, compressed bulk files, updates, cancellations, 429s, restarts). The format of the FAA's real bulk files isn't documented, so the reader accepts gzip or plain JSON in several layouts. If the first download fails, check the server log.
- METAR and TAF field names were checked against live AWC responses on 2026-11-19. CWA field names weren't (none were active), so CWA text uses fallbacks.
